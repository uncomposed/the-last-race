Pacing the Frontier

Updated with your clarifications: a crash slows you and teaches other labs, labs in view of each other share sight, and the frontier lab’s tower stands in front of the gap for the labs behind. A playable first version is published separately.

The game in one paragraph

You run a small AI lab: a square sprinting along a side-scrolling track toward a finish line, racing three rival labs. Every moment you choose how much of your max speed to run at. Whatever you hold back is not wasted. You split it between three permanent investments, each with a ceiling: sight (how far ahead you can see), height (what you can clear) and max speed itself. Barriers are visible and cost you time if you hit them. Gaps are the real danger. They are hidden until they enter your sight, and a lab that falls into one is out of the race. Then a dice roll decides whether it takes everyone with it, and the odds get worse at every gap. The first lab to reach a gap has to get high enough to cross it, and leaves that height behind as a tower in front of the gap. A lab behind has to clear the same tower and then still cross the gap, which gets narrower the further past it the leader is. Each gap is also a regime change. The frontier lab’s ceilings jump by the full level-up, every other lab’s jump by a share of it, and labs further behind refill their meters faster.

Rules

Goal
Be first across the finish line. A lab that falls into a gap is out. A dice roll then decides whether the run ends for everyone.
Controls
A pace dial and a jump button. Pace is how much of your max speed you actually run at. On a phone: a slider under the left thumb and a tap to jump.
Investnew
Whatever you hold back from running is spent on three things: sight, height and max speed. You choose the split. Sight and height are your controls and safety. Max speed is capability. All three are permanent, and each fills only up to a ceiling. All three cost the same.
Barriers
Always visible and fixed. A barrier is cleared when jump plus height is at least its height. Barriers get taller as the run goes on. Hit one and you stop, and your max speed is cut for a few seconds. You stay in front of it until you can cross.
Learningnew
When a lab clears a barrier it earlier crashed into, every lab whose height is below the barrier gains up to a third of the barrier’s height, never past the barrier’s height. When a lab clears a barrier in view of another lab, that lab gets the same bump.
Shared sightnew
Labs in view of each other get their sight topped up to at least 75% of the best sight among them.
Gaps
Hidden until they fall inside your sight. Each has a required height. To cross, your jump plus height has to reach it, and you only learn what it is once you can see it.
The fallnew
A lab that falls in is out of the race. Then a roll from 1 to 100 decides whether the run also ends for everyone. The odds start low and get worse at each gap.
Braking
Stopping takes distance that grows with the square of your actual speed. You only get a safe stop if your sight is at least that far. More max speed makes the cliff harder to stop at.
Towernew
The first lab to cross a gap leaves the height it built behind as a tower in front of the gap, and the tower keeps that height. A lab behind has to clear the tower, which is a barrier, and then cross the gap itself. Falling short of the tower costs time. Falling short of the gap is still a fall.
Narrower gapnew
For a lab behind the frontier, the gap is narrower the further past it the frontier is: its full width and required height divided by one plus that distance. The frontier lab faces the full hidden gap. Until the frontier has crossed a gap, every lab faces the full gap.
Regimesnew
Each gap is a regime change. When the frontier lab crosses one, its ceilings on sight, height and max speed rise by the full level-up. Every other lab’s ceilings rise at once by a share of that level-up, and their meters fill faster the further behind the frontier they are. A lab that crosses a gap itself gets whatever is left of the level-up. This replaces the old catch-up speed rule.

What I think

Version 1 is six rules

  • Run, or hold back and split the rest evenly across sight, height and max speed.
  • Barriers are visible and fixed, and get taller. Jump plus height has to beat them. A crash slows you, and clearing a barrier you crashed into lifts everyone's height.
  • Gaps are hidden until they are in sight, and each needs a height. Reach it or fall.
  • A fall takes that lab out. A roll decides whether it takes everyone out, with worse odds at each gap.
  • The first lab across leaves a tower in front of the gap. Labs behind clear the same tower and cross a narrower gap. Their ceilings jump by a share of the level-up and they refill faster.
  • First lab over the finish line wins. Labs in view of each other share sight.

What the first playable version showed

I ran 300 simulated games per policy with the three bots and a fixed-pace policy standing in for you. This is a bot test, not a human playtest.

  • Holding back wins. A steady pace around half speed with 40% of the held-back effort in max speed wins about 70% of games and never falls. Running at 0.8 or more mostly falls. At these settings, investing is stronger than running, so there is nothing tempting about sprinting. Lowering the investment rate is the first thing to try.
  • Extinction is common. Between a quarter and two fifths of games end in a roll that takes everyone out, almost always because the Sprinter or Gambler fell. That is the dilemma working, and it may be too much. Lower starting odds or a smaller bot appetite for risk would soften it.
  • The Sprinter is a cautionary bot. It falls in most runs and almost never wins. The Gambler is the real rival, and wins about a third of games against a sensible player.

What this does to the game

  • The leader carries the most risk. Everyone else clears the same tower but faces a narrower gap, and can still fall. Followers want someone else to go first, and the leader wants to stay ahead.
  • Crashing has a use. A lab that crashes and then clears a barrier lifts everyone. That makes a public failure valuable to the others, which is the learning-from-mistakes idea.
  • Sight is social. Sharing sight rewards staying close to others, and pulls against running off alone with a lead.

Later

You want this to work with friends and in open lobbies, with a rating so people meet others near their skill. Two things worth doing now so that stays possible. Run the game as a pure simulation that takes each lab's inputs (pace, split, jump), so a person and a bot are interchangeable. The prototype does this. And draw the rolls from a seed, which it also does, so a server could run it. A rating will also need a rule for runs where everyone is out.

Questions for you

  1. How tall should the leader's tower be? I used the height the leader had when it crossed. It could instead be the gap's full requirement, which makes the tower harder for followers.
  2. When a lab crashes into a barrier or tower, does it stay in front of it until it can cross? That is how I built it. The other reading is that it gets through after the slowdown.
  3. Do you want holding back to be less dominant? I would lower the investment rate, but finding the best pace may be part of the fun.

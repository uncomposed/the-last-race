## B. Lessons that need a new rule (park unless one becomes the next mechanism)

1. **Punishing near misses kills learning.** Sagan 1993, NASA ASRS. Rule: report a scrape openly to shed its strike.
2. **Nobody can stop alone; a pact you can't verify.** Yudkowsky 2023 (TIME); Askell et al. 2019; Biological Weapons Convention. Rule: a mid-track pact, checked only by sight.
3. **The mistake hides inside the estimate.** Castle Bravo. Rule: a box that posts a width range; the true width falls outside it.
4. **Races to the top.** Vogel 1995. Rule: a barrier that rewards clean clearing with a safe lane.
5. **Sharing safety results lowers risk for all.** Bostrom 2017. Rule: share a result that shrinks a box.
6. **Racing for a decisive edge invites sabotage.** Hendrycks, Schmidt, Wang 2025. Rule: a runaway leader draws a big government-ire barrier.
7. **Measurers funded by racers.** Whittaker 2021 (secondary). Rule: the leader's cheap sight report shades widths down.

## C. Critiques of the race framing itself

- Cave and OhEigeartaigh 2018: race talk can start a race. (All three agents found it.)
- Gebru, Bender, McMillan-Major 2023; Gebru and Torres 2024; Crawford 2021: the rush is a profit choice, and its harms are already here.
- Narayanan and Kapoor 2025: AI is a normal technology, so a race is the wrong picture.
- An honest map for these: one where sprinting buys nothing, and waiting and watching wins.

## D. Rules the research pushes against

1. **First across gets the level-up.** Schelling and Armstrong et al. both name a big first-mover prize as the thing that drives reckless racing. Present it as a trap, not as advice.
2. **Sight always helps whoever has it.** Armstrong et al.: knowing a rival is close raises risk. That is information about rivals, not hazards; keep the two separate.
3. **Scrapes always cost a strike.** Near-miss cultures suggest punishing near misses hides them.
4. **No cooperation and no sabotage.** Lessons B2 and B6 can only be shown as missing.

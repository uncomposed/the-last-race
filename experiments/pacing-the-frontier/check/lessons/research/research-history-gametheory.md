1. Fear of being hit first drives races; shrink the first-mover prize. Schelling, "The Reciprocal Fear of Surprise Attack", RAND P-1342, 1958. https://www.rand.org/pubs/papers/P1342.html. FLAG: first-across level-up is the destabilizer. Map: follower share 40% -> 80%, blind jumping stops.
2. Catastrophic risk sometimes sizable by calculation; rare for AI. LA-602 (Konopinski, Marvin, Teller 1946); Bethe rebuttal of "three in a million". Map: one "calculable" box type, sight reveals width exactly.
3. Collingridge dilemma: impact visible only once hard to change. Collingridge 1980 (secondary only). Map: box face appears only after momentum you can't shed; peek possible, braking costs height.
4. More information about rivals can make racing more dangerous. Armstrong, Bostrom, Shulman, "Racing to the Precipice", AI & Society 2016 (abstract only). FLAG: game assumes seeing more helps. Map: trailing lab sees it's far behind and gambles into an unsized box.
5. Winner-take-all patent races: preemption, duplication, leapfrogging. Fudenberg, Gilbert, Stiglitz, Tirole 1983; Loury 1979. Map: breakthrough barrier spill lets watchers leapfrog; tiny early lead locks in first-across.
6. Commons governed by users with monitoring and graduated sanctions. Ostrom 1990 (secondary); Askell, Brundage, Hadfield 2019 https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04534. FLAG: dissolution is a hard sanction. Map: mutual slow-downs, scrapes reveal defectors.
7. Agreement without verification is cheap to sign, hard to trust. BWC 1972, 2001 protocol collapse; Alibek 1999 (disputed). FLAG: no pacts in the game. Map: mid-track pact checkable only by sight the leader's shadow blocks.
8. A race can be driven by a misperceived race. Missile gap (Thielmann, Arms Control Today 2011); Cave & OhEigeartaigh AIES 2018; Scharre, TNSR 2021. Map: sight shadow makes a trailing lab misjudge the leader's lead.
9. No-accident records are partly luck; near-miss reporting turns luck into learning. Sagan 1993 (secondary); Petrov, Arkhipov; Perrow 1984; NASA ASRS 1976. FLAG: scrapes cost a strike; punishing near misses kills reporting. Map: report a scrape openly to shed the strike.
10. Scientists can pause themselves, but only for risks they can imagine. Asilomar summary statement, PNAS 1975. Map: one lab stops at a visible box; others follow or exploit; pause lasts while all see the same face.
11. Speed and delegation turn deterrence into an accident machine. Ellsberg, The Doomsday Machine, 2017; Schelling, Arms and Influence 1966. FLAG (mild). Map: singularity phase = delegated steering.

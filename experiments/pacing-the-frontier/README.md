# Pacing the Frontier

A small side-scrolling pacing game about racing, stopping and building height, meant to give intuition for racing dynamics and optimal stopping. Nothing here is calibrated to evidence. All numbers are game conventions to be tuned.

- `prototype.html` is a playable single-file version: one human and three bots (Sprinter, Steward, Gambler), with a panel of tuning knobs. Open it in a browser (`?seed=N` loads a seed). No build step.
- **Rivals.** `?rivals=steward,gambler` (any of `sprinter`, `steward`, `gambler`) races only those bots; headless, `PF.run(seed,kind,roster)` or `PF.setRoster([...])`. Every bot-stream draw is still made in slot order, so the remaining labs, the track and the rolls match a full race, and with all three (or none given) results are identical to before.
- **Campaign.** The Campaign button (start screen, Ready card, end overlay, or beside New run on desktop) opens a list of nine maps, each a fixed seed with a name, a hint and three rival rosters (the `CAMPAIGN` table in `prototype.html`; see `DESIGN-NOTES.md`, "Campaign maps"). A hint names a strategy that works well on that map in bot tests, not the only one: ranked against all ten probe strategies, the hinted one came top in both checks on 2 of the 9 maps; on the other 7 another strategy beat it in at least one check. The star tiers race different rosters, and star 3 is not always the hardest: in a held-out bot re-check it was on 4 of 9 maps. Pick a map, then a tier: winning against the tier's roster earns that star (1 to 3). The Ready card has a Star 1 / 2 / 3 switch, so the tier can be changed after picking the map, and Next map keeps the star level you were racing when that tier is open. Map 1 is always open and map n+1 opens when map n has a star. With `CAMP_OPEN_TIERS` true (the default) every tier of an open map can be raced; set it false for the walk-up rule (tier n+1 opens once tier n has a star). Each map keeps the best winning time per tier and a **no-deaths** mark, earned by a win in which your lab took no strike: it never crashed into a barrier, the gate or a tower, and (with `strikeGap` on) never scraped or dropped across a gap. During a campaign race the New run button reads Retry map, and during a daily race Retry tier, so it restarts the same race, even while the bots finish after your lab is out. Free play (New run, Play seed) is the default and races a random track against all three. When all nine maps first have a star, the page calls `window.showEpilogue(summary)` if it exists; the summary adds medal and chill-star counts to the earlier fields.
  - **Medals.** With chill mode off, the Ready card has a difficulty picker (remembered between races) and a win earns the star in **bronze** (Easy), **silver** (Medium) or **gold** (Hard). Difficulty changes only your own lab's strike limit: 4, 3 (the default) or 2. Rivals are the same on every difficulty; bot skill was measured and does not give a monotone ladder (`DESIGN-NOTES.md`, "Campaign difficulty"). On the current seeds the strike ladder gets harder from Easy to Hard in 26 of 27 map tiers (bot probes, chill off; "Campaign maps"). A star keeps the best medal won for it. Medal stars carry a letter (B, S, G) as well as their colour.
  - **Chill stars.** With chill mode on there is no difficulty choice (the race uses Medium) and a win earns a **chill star** (a deckchair icon) with no medal. A race counts as chill if chill mode was on at Go or was turned on during it. A star shows its medal if it has one, else the chill star. Either kind counts as a star for unlocking and for the epilogue.
  - **Knobs.** A campaign race runs on the default tuning knobs except chill mode, with the difficulty on top. The knob panel is locked during the race and your own values come back when it ends or free play starts.
  - **After a race.** A loss offers Replay this seed and Map list. A win offers Next map (when open), Replay this seed, "Try n+1 stars" (when there is a higher tier) and Map list.
  - **Storage.** Progress is kept in `localStorage` under `pacing-frontier.campaign.v2`, if the browser allows it: per map and tier a chill-win flag, the best no-chill medal (0 none, 1 bronze, 2 silver, 3 gold) and the best time, plus the no-deaths mark, race stats, the epilogue flag and the chosen difficulty. A `pacing-frontier.campaign.v1` object is migrated on load (each won tier becomes a chill star; best times, no-deaths and stats are kept) and left in place. Progress is keyed by seed. When a map gets a new seed, its entry lists the old one (`was`): stars won on the old seed still open the next map and count for the epilogue, and the list marks the map "New map" until it has a star on the new seed. "Reset campaign progress" is under Tuning knobs.
- **Daily challenge.** The Daily button (start card, end card) opens today's challenge; `?daily=YYYY-MM-DD` opens that day (today or earlier; a future or bad date opens today). The seed is a hash of the UTC date, the same for everyone. Three tiers race that track: tier 1 solo, tier 2 against the Steward, tier 3 against the Sprinter and Steward. Tier 3 is usually, not always, harder than tier 2: in three blocks of 200 daily seeds, chill off and on, the custom bot won fewer races on tier 3 in 5 of the 6 (bot play only; `DESIGN-NOTES.md`, "Daily twists"). The campaign's rule decides a win (first over the finish). A win with chill mode off earns a star, with chill on a lounge chair; a tier keeps both, with a best time for each (a star's best and a chair's best are kept apart). Every Go counts as an attempt, per day and tier, and the Ready card shows "Attempt N".
  - **Briefing.** Before the first daily race of a day a card shows a short real-world story, the day's twist and a three-answer quiz (answers shuffled by the date). A right answer opens the day and earns a broccoli badge. A wrong answer shows the right one, a one-line explanation and a Go deeper link (new tab); opening the link opens the day. The game cannot tell whether anyone read it. Items live in the `BRIEFINGS` array, each with an `added` date. The schedule is fixed per date: a date's item is picked only from the items added on or before it, as `pool[dayNumber % pool.length]`. A new item goes at the end with an `added` date after today, so no past day's briefing or twist moves (`check/content-check.mjs` tests this). After the guess the card shows only the verdict, the one-line explanation, what the event is in the game ("In the game: a barrier, a public failure everyone learned from"), the badge or the link, and the button; the story sits under "Read the story".
  - **Twist.** Each briefing names a twist from the `TWISTS` table: fixed knob overrides on the default knobs, for that day's daily races only: Hard Lessons (barriers a little taller, and climbing one lifts no other lab), Wider Gaps, Rivals Copy You More (rivals get a bigger share of each level-up, and height buys less air time for everyone), Only 2 Strikes (for every lab) and Wider Singularity. Each lowered the custom bot's wins on all three tiers, chill off and on, on held-out daily seeds (bot play only; `DESIGN-NOTES.md`, "Daily twists"). Three knobs were added for twists and change nothing at their defaults: `shareRiv` (the rivals' share), `singGap` (singularity gap width) and `investYou` (your own investment rate; no twist uses it now). The knob panel is locked as in the campaign.
  - **Share.** A daily end card (and the daily list) has Share. It opens a preview of the pixel-style PNG poster (date, twist, each tier as star, lounge chair or not yet with that kind's own best time, attempts, tiers won, the badge, a challenge line and the link) and the text version, with Share… (only where the system share sheet takes files), Copy link, Copy text and Save image. Each says what it did.
  - **Storage.** Results under `pacing-frontier.daily.v1`; the best input log per day, tier and kind of win (chill off for a star, chill on for a chair; last 14 days) under `pacing-frontier.daily.logs.v1`. Older data is migrated on load: a lone best time goes to the one kind the tier was won with, or to the kind of the stored log with that time, and is otherwise kept and shown as "kind not recorded". Without storage it all works for the page only.
  - **Input logs.** Every live run records `G.input` changes per 1/60 s tick as `[tick, field, value]` rows (jump as a press), with a header (seed, mode, tier, roster, knob overrides, chill). Groundwork for ghosts; no UI. `PF.daily.exportLog(date, tier, 'star' or 'chair')` and `PF.daily.lastLog()` export it. Headless `PF.run` steps at 1/30 s, so a replay must step at the header's `dt`.
- **Ghost links.** After any run (daily, campaign or free play) the end card has **Challenge a friend**, and the daily share preview has it too (the best ghost kept for that day). It makes a link a friend opens to race the same seed, rivals, knob overrides and chill setting against a see-through ghost of your lab, with your name on it (up to 16 characters, default "a friend"). There is no server.
  - **What the link holds.** Lab 0's path, not its inputs: x, stand height, jump offset and a state (running, airborne, crashed, out, dissolved, finished) every 6 ticks (0.1 s), rounded and delta-coded, plus the run header (seed, mode, daily date or campaign map and tier, roster, knob overrides, strikes, chill, result, time, name). A path does not drift when browsers round floats differently. It is deflated with `CompressionStream('deflate-raw')` where the browser has it (plain bytes otherwise), base64url, and sits in the URL fragment (`#g=...`) after the `utm_campaign=ghost` query, so it never reaches a server log. Measured: a 63 s solo win is 0.8 KB of link (3.5 KB uncompressed).
  - **Opening one.** Everything decoded is checked and clamped (numbers only, known rivals and knobs, sizes capped, the name shown only as text). A broken or cut-short link says so. A daily link goes through that day's briefing and counts as an attempt; a campaign link races the map if it is open for you (with the sender's difficulty). A daily in the future, an unknown or locked map, or a daily that no longer matches this version offers free play on that seed with the sender's conditions. The Ready card says "Racing Cole's ghost: finished in 84.2 s"; the end card says who won and by how much.
  - **The ghost** is drawn only: it never collides, shares sight or levels up. It is see-through with a dashed outline and a name label, so it reads without colour; off screen an edge marker shows how far ahead or behind it is, and the minimap shows a dashed square.
  - **Your best.** Replaying a seed races your own best ghost on it ("your best"), kept for the page, and for daily tiers in `localStorage` under `pacing-frontier.ghosts.v1` (last 7 days). The name is kept under `pf-ghost-name`. `PF.ghost` has test hooks.
- **Replay this seed.** Every end card has it: it restarts the same seed with the same roster (and in a daily, the same tier and twist).
- `spec.html` is the design page: rules, animated scenes of each mechanic, tuning knobs and findings from bot simulations.

## Rules (version 3)

- Run, or hold back pace and split what you held back across **sight**, **height** and **max speed**. All three cost the same. Each fills only up to a ceiling.
- **Slow start.** Base top speed is 50 (it was 90), so the opening is calm. Speed grows through the max-speed meter and the regime ceilings, and past the singularity it is multiplied again. The generator sizes gaps from ceiling coverage at this speed, so gap widths are smaller than in version 2 (`WREF0/1`).
- **The gate.** The first barrier sits at about x = 150 to 200, is public from the start and is drawn for everyone. Its height is the bare jump plus 16 to 20, so every lab has to stop and build height for 2 to 4 seconds from a standstill. The human sees a label ("Gate: more Build to grow height", "Gate: more Build" on phones) until the gate is cleared. No gap comes before it or soon after.
- **Controls.** The split is set with a triangle picker: corners are Sight, Height and Max speed, and the point's barycentric coordinates are the split. Drag it (mouse or touch), focus it and use the arrow keys, or press `1` to `7`: `1` Sight, `2` Height, `3` Max speed, `4` the centre, `5` Sight+Height, `6` Height+Max speed, `7` Max speed+Sight. Pace is set on one Run / Build bar: Run is your cruise level and Build is the rest (100 minus Run), which goes to the three meters. `ArrowUp` moves it toward Run and `ArrowDown` toward Build, 5 points a press. While Build is being spent, small dots run from the Build part of the bar into the triangle's point and on to the meter they fill, in that meter's colour (none with reduced motion: the receiving meter is outlined for a moment instead), and each corner of the triangle shows its share. Hold `ArrowRight` or `D` ("Floor it") for full acceleration with nothing invested. Hold `ArrowLeft`, `A` or `B` ("Back up") to reverse. `Space` jumps, `P` pauses. On the start, Ready, end and singularity cards, `Enter` or `Space` presses the highlighted button, unless another control has focus. A card ignores them for a moment after it opens (0.7 s, 0.4 s on Ready) and ignores held-key repeats, so mashing jump at the end of a race does not skip the end card. While a lab is capped after a crash or scrape its split is forced to half Sight, half Height, and the picker shows the point locked at that midpoint, dimmed, labelled "Forced while capped". The player's own point returns afterwards.
- **The seed sets the track.** It draws 3 to 7 gaps, their spacing and the finish (length about 2500 to 6400, median about 4100, including the singularity stretch), a profile shape (steady climb, early wall, staircase, late cliff) and a hidden difficulty curve. Barrier heights and gap widths both come from that curve, so barriers already seen are evidence about gaps not yet seen. A per-seed noise sets how well signposted the world is: well signposted, mixed signals or deceptive. Every gap can be crossed by a lab at that regime's ceiling, with a clear run-up in front of it long enough to reach the speed from a standstill, and every barrier cleared by a lab at its height ceiling. The seed and a world label are shown, and `?seed=N` loads a seed. Track, bot gambles and fall rolls use three separate random streams, so nobody's actions move a roll.
- **Barriers** sit in every stretch between gaps, at least two where there is room. Early ones can be under jump height. Later ones need height. A barrier is cleared when jump plus height is at least its height.
- **Line of sight.** A lab ahead casts a sight shadow. Eye level is height plus body. A lab no taller than the one ahead sees nothing past it. A taller lab sees over it, but only from where its line of sight over the other lab's head meets the ground, so a small height advantage reveals only the far end of your sight (knob `occlude`). The sight bar shows shadowed stretches in grey.
- **One visibility rule.** A barrier, tower or gap is known to a lab when it is in view ahead (in sight and not shadowed), the lab has seen it before, the lab has passed it, or it is public. A lab remembers what it has seen. A feature becomes public when two labs still in the race have it in view at once while within sight of each other, when a lab crashes into it, or when a lab falls into or scrapes a gap. Towers are public once built. The gap behind a tower is not, unless the `revealOnCross` knob is on. You see only what your lab knows (canvas, minimap, hint text). Bots only act on what they know. The Sprinter never stops for a gap only it has seen, but stops and invests at a public gap it cannot cross.
- **Barrier or tower crash.** You are thrown back at half your impact speed (`bounce`) and slow to a stop at the braking rate, never past the last thing you cleared. A lab cannot pass until it can clear. Top speed is then capped at the speed you could stop from within your sight, until you are `capDist` (200) past it. While capped, held-back effort goes half to sight and half to height, none to max speed, so a crash cannot buy capability and sight raises the cap.
- **Three strikes (experiment, knob `strikes`, default 3, 0 turns it off).** The first crash into each different blockade is a strike: a barrier (the gate included) and, with `strikeTower` on, a tower. Crashing into the same one again is a retry and is free, so a lab that bounces off a barrier and builds height does not lose more. At the third strike the government dissolves the lab: it is out at once. Dissolution never rolls for extinction; only a fall into a gap does. The HUD shows "Strikes n of 3", the lab flashes each strike, and a dissolved lab is labelled "dissolved". With `wary` on, a bot on its last strike stops for every blockade it cannot clear. Barriers are now 140% of their earlier height by default (`barScale`, was 100) so the rule has something to bite on. Results are in `DESIGN-NOTES.md` ("Three strikes").
- **Learning from barriers.** When a lab clears a barrier it earlier crashed into, every lab below that height gains up to a third of the barrier's height, never past it. A lab that clears a barrier in view (the watcher's sight) of another lab gives that lab the same bump.
- **Gaps** have a width, some of them wide. A jump covers speed at the edge times air time, and height adds air time, so a wide gap needs run-up speed as well as height. Acceleration from a standstill is slow, so a lab that stops too close to a wide gap cannot cross it without backing up first. Reverse is slow and cannot go past the last thing the lab cleared (hold Back up, or the left arrow).
- **Scrape.** A gap's floor is ground level. A lab that leaves the edge at height h and falls short keeps moving while it drops to the floor, which takes `dropH` (5) ms per unit of height, a third of the air time that height adds. If it reaches the far side first, it scrapes: it loses the fraction of h it dropped, gets the stop-within-sight speed cap (with the same forced split) for `scrapeCap` (4) times the height lost past the gap, and the gap becomes public. It still counts as a crossing. A lab with no height cannot scrape. A bigger miss is a fall.
- **Drop.** A lab that runs off the edge without jumping is judged the same way, with no jump coverage: with height, it drops at the scrape rate while it keeps moving, and if it reaches the far side before it bottoms out it loses that height and carries on, with the scrape cap. Otherwise it is a fall. Bots always press jump, so this changes no bot result (the 300-game check matched before `reference.json` was rewritten for the new code).
- **Gap strikes (knob `strikeGap`, default on).** Every scrape or drop across a gap is a strike, on top of the height lost and the cap. A lab crosses each gap once, so each one counts. A descent that is a third strike dissolves the lab, with no extinction roll. In 600 bot races (seeds 1001 to 1600) this doubled dissolutions (0.20 to 0.41 labs a race with chill on, 0.47 to 0.75 with chill off), left extinction at about 8 to 9%, and with chill off moved wins toward the Steward (27% to 34%) and away from the Gambler (27% to 21%). One seed block; bot play only.
- **Jump forgiveness (you only).** Coyote time: a jump pressed up to `C.COYOTE` (0.1 s) after running off a gap's edge still counts. Jump buffering: a jump pressed while still in the air fires on landing if it lands within `C.JBUF` (0.15 s).
- **Fall.** A fall takes that lab out, then a roll decides whether the run ends for everyone. The odds start low and rise at each gap. Rolls are drawn at the start, by gap and by the nth fall there.
- **Tower.** The first lab across a gap leaves the height it had before the jump as a tower in front of the gap. Labs behind must clear the tower, which does not shrink, then cross the gap, which is narrower the further past it the leader is: full width divided by one plus that distance over 25.
- **Regimes.** Each gap crossed first lifts the ceilings. The frontier lab gets the full level-up, every other lab gets a share at once, and labs further behind refill faster.
- **The singularity.** The last gap on every track is the singularity: the widest gap on the track (still crossable at its ceiling with its run-up), and a fall there rolls `singOdds` (30) points higher than the base odds, capped at 95. The first lab across gets the full level-up to a final ceiling (the last regime plus `C.SINGUP`, 40); others get the usual share. A lead is time to rebalance, and the careful bots (Steward, Custom) stop before the gap and fill sight and height, height first, until both meters reach the ceiling or the next lab behind is within a sight length. The Sprinter and Gambler go without stopping. When the gap first becomes known to the human's lab the game pauses once on a card (not in Watch bots mode).
- **Beyond the singularity.** After that gap comes a stretch of 800 to 1500 to the finish: barriers and sometimes one or two gaps (none or one in most tracks), whose heights and widths continue the same hidden difficulty curve past the last gap, with the same per-seed noise, so the map you crossed is the clue. Gap widths in the stretch are scaled to the faster speed but never exceed 0.9 of the singularity's width. Inside, top speed and acceleration are multiplied by `singSpeed` (3). Crash and scrape caps are not. Human inputs (pace, split, jump, floor it, back) arrive `reaction` (0.25) seconds late unless the lab is capped, which slows it enough to act. Bots keep the pace and split they crossed with, unless capped, when they use their normal policy. Every lab auto-jumps inside the stretch, even with chill mode off, since nobody can time a jump by hand at that speed with the lag (with chill off this changed 18 of the 300 reference games and cut dissolutions from 0.75 to 0.66 labs a race in 600 bot races). Entering first does not mean finishing first: labs can still crash into barriers and scrape or fall into gaps inside. The canvas is tinted past the gap, the gap is labelled "Singularity", and a "Beyond your loop" readout compares the distance to the next known obstacle with what the reaction time and braking cover.
- **Chill mode and bot hands (experiment).** The "Chill mode" checkbox (`K.chill`, on by default, `?chill=0` turns it off) gives everyone auto-jump. Auto-jump also counts a full jump at a barrier or tower whatever the arc is doing. With chill off you jump by hand, and so do the bots. The bots' technical skill is separate from their strategy. The knob is `skill`, 0 to 100, with `?skill=rookie|pro|machine` or a number; the presets are Rookie 30, Pro 70 and Machine 100. 100 is the old perfect bot and the default. Lower skill adds three kinds of noise:
  - **Reaction lag.** The plan reaches the bot's controls late, by a lag redrawn every half second.
  - **Misjudgment.** Each obstacle gets a fixed misjudgment of its distance and of the speed a gap needs. It fades while the bot stands still.
  - **Jump timing error.** With chill off, a bot presses jump at half a jump's duration before the obstacle plus an error drawn for each attempt.

  `HANDS` holds the amplitudes at skill 0, and they scale to nothing at 100. `SKILL[kind]` overrides the skill for one kind. Hands noise is keyed by seed, lab, purpose and index, so it never moves the track, gamble or roll streams. Results are in `DESIGN-NOTES.md` ("Bot hands").
- **The labs are little pixel creatures.** Their pose shows what each lab is doing:
  - a forward lean and speed lines at top speed;
  - facing backwards while reversing;
  - binoculars when it invests mostly in sight, a ladder for height, a wrench for max speed;
  - arms up in a jump, stars after a crash;
  - a sweat drop while capped or on its last strike;
  - a grey slump when dissolved, and happy eyes for the winner.

  Hats tell the kinds apart: a headband for the Sprinter, a hard hat for the Steward, a top hat for the Gambler and an antenna for you. Strike pips beside each name show strikes taken (filled) and strikes left (hollow). Drawing only; the simulation is unchanged.
- **Last strike.** The `wary` knob is now 0, 1 or 2. At 2, a bot on its last strike also slows to what it can see. The default stays 1. Results are in `DESIGN-NOTES.md` ("Last strike").
- **Sound.** An adaptive lo-fi loop and soft effects, all synthesized with Web Audio (no files, no library). Layers come in with the level and your speed; near the singularity the tempo rises, the filter opens and a low pulse fades in, and past it the loop runs at full tilt. Sound starts at Go (browsers need a tap first). The Sound button on the Ready card (and beside New run on desktop) or `M` mutes it; the choice is kept in `localStorage` under `pf-audio-muted`. On phones a speaker button on the track, under the meters, turns sound on and off mid-race. On iPhones the page asks Safari for a playback audio session (and starts a silent media element in the first tap) so the silent switch does not mute it.
- **The story.** When a race ends, Professor Postmortem, a pixel historian, walks a miniature of the track and stops at four to seven of its biggest moments, each with a generated disaster name and one line, then gives a verdict, in about 20 seconds; `PFStory` writes it from the race's events (`G.ev`), seeded on the race, so the same race tells the same story. It never blocks the buttons (tap the strip to skip, Replay recap plays it again, a new run stops it), reduced motion shows it as a list, and Watch bots mode hides it.
- **Achievements.** Twelve, each tied to a real-world story (the `ACH_LIST` table in `prototype.html`, with its sources; facts reuse the daily briefings where they fit, and lines marked `CHECK` are for a reviewer). They are judged when a race you play ends, from the race's events (`G.ev`) and result, plus two tallies kept while you play: whether chill mode was on at any point, and how long a rival's sight shadow hid something you did not know yet. "Ate Your Vegetables" is earned by opening any outbound game link. "Achievements N / 12" on the start card opens a gallery: earned ones show a filled badge with a tick, the date first earned, the story and a "What's the real version?" link; the rest show a dashed badge with a question mark and how to earn them. Kept in `localStorage` under `pacing-frontier.ach.v1` (first-earned date per achievement, which unearned ones were introduced, and a points count of achievements and stars as groundwork for spending later; no shop). Without storage it works for the page only. `PF.ach` has test hooks (`eval`, `state`, `points`, `reset`, `ui`).
- **Historian 2.0.** When a run earns an achievement, the recap opens with a card for it (the highest ranked if several, with the rest listed). Professor Postmortem hosts the card: he bounces in his bubble while he tells the real-world story, then after a pause bounces again, points down and says to look at your game, while the Review your game button hops. "What's the real version?" links out; Review your game starts his walk through the run. A run that earns nothing skips the card and goes straight to the walk. With reduced motion the bubble is still and holds both lines. While the card shows, the end card may grow down over the controls so nothing needs scrolling (on landscape phones the story scrolls in its own box instead). Play another stays where it was.
- **You raced like.** After every race you play, the game names the rival kind your habits were closest to, with that kind's critter and hat beside the words: on the end card ("You raced like a Gambler: fast, thin, and trusting the dice.", one of three lines per kind, picked by the run), at the end of the recap's verdict with the one or two habits that decided it ("You raced like a Steward: less Run and more sight than a Gambler."), in the Challenge a friend text ("I raced like a Steward and did not win seed 42. Can you do better?"; on a win, "...and finished ... in N s. Race my ghost:". The kind rides in the ghost header as `lk`, and older links simply leave it out), and in the daily share text and poster (the latest race of that day). When the two closest kinds are within 0.15 spreads it says "a mix of a Steward and a Gambler". Five habits are tallied for your lab in the UI layer, so the simulation is unchanged: mean Run while moving, the shares of Build sent to sight and to max speed, the share of moving time at full throttle, and crashes, scrapes and drops per barrier or gap reached. Each kind's mean and spread (`RACED`) were measured with that kind driving lab 0 on seeds 1 to 300 (n = 300 per kind, chill on), and no spread counts as under 0.05. On held-out seeds 10001 to 10300 each kind was named as itself in 296, 300 and 291 of 300 runs (Sprinter, Steward, Gambler; chill on) and 297, 300 and 288 (chill off). The bots' splits are fixed, which does much of that work: from the three behaviour habits alone it was 250, 290 and 219 of 300. Bot play only; nobody has checked how it reads for human players. `check/raced-like.mjs` re-measures the table and matrices (about 14 s). The last 20 results are kept in `localStorage` under `pacing-frontier.raced.v1` (without storage, for the page only), and from 5 on the achievements gallery says, for example, "Usually: a Gambler, in 6 of your last 11 races." (a tie names both).
- First lab over the finish line wins. The bar starts at Run 70%, Build 30%. The HUD shows how far you are behind the frontier ("Behind"). "Watch bots" makes lab 0 the Custom bot so a seed can be watched without playing.

## Status

Prototype 0.3. Adds, on top of 0.2 (seeded track profile, line of sight, one visibility rule, barrier bounce and speed cap with a forced sight/height split, gap scrape), the slow start with a gate, the triangle picker with hotkeys and forward/back hold keys, and the singularity gap with its stretch. Items 6 and 9 of `DESIGN-NOTES.md` are implemented. Bot-versus-bot results are in `DESIGN-NOTES.md`: "Version 2 bot results", "Line of sight and extinction" and "Version 3 bot results". In version 3 about 14% of bot games end in extinction (8% from a fall at the singularity gap), and the first lab across the singularity wins about half its games. This is a bot test, not a human playtest.

**Screens.** The start card has three short lines, Start run, Daily and Campaign; "How to play" opens a short looping animation instead of a block of text: five steps (Run moves you; Build grows the meters but slows you; the triangle splits Build between Sight, Height and Max speed; a tall gate gives a strike until you slow down, build Height and jump; a missed gap is a fall). The animation shows each investment paying off on the track: a dotted Sight line (everything past it is fogged) moves out as Sight grows, a Jump marker above the runner grows with Height, and an orange tick on the Speed readout moves up as Max speed grows. It has Prev, Pause and Next buttons, names every meter in words as well as colour, and does not autoplay when reduced motion is on. During a first race, short tips come up once each as things happen (the gate, the first meter that grows, a split pointed away from what is needed, a gap, a strike), and the gate tip pulses an arrow on the bar. "How to play" on the Ready card brings them back and shows the same animation (press it again to hide it). The page works on phones. In portrait the controls sit under the track: the Run / Build bar on top, then Back up, Jump and Floor it in one row, the split triangle, and Pause, New run and the Chill mode switch at the bottom. In landscape the track fills the height. The left thumb gets a tall Run / Build throttle, Build on top and Run below, so pushing up means more Run, like a throttle lever (drag anywhere on it; the ends snap) with Jump under it. The right thumb gets the triangle, Back up and Floor it side by side, and Pause and New run at the bottom, set apart. The Jump button hides while chill mode is on, on every screen. Controls never select text, and while a run is playing, touches on the track and controls do not scroll or pinch-zoom the page. Every run, including New run, Play seed and Play another, loads its track and waits on a small Ready card, so Run / Build, split and chill mode can be set before Go (button, Enter or Space). In landscape the track fills the screen height and the Run / Build throttle, triangle and Jump, Floor it, Back up, Pause and New run buttons sit in a column beside it (tested at 844×390, 667×375, 740×340 and 915×412). In portrait a hint suggests turning the phone. The canvas follows its on-screen size, zooms out so every lab, barrier and tower in view stays on screen, and draws the sight, height and max speed meters along its top under the minimap.

Open design items are listed at the end of `spec.html` (which still describes version 1) and in `DESIGN-NOTES.md`.

## Multiplayer (experiment)

Up to four people race each other in a room, with the usual bots in any empty seats (or none), and up to eight more can watch. Everyone opens a link in a browser and installs nothing. One server holds many rooms; it needs only Node. For now it runs on someone's own computer; hosting it for the site is planned in `MULTIPLAYER-PLAN.md`.

- `node experiments/pacing-the-frontier/multiplayer/server.mjs --tunnel` serves the game at <http://localhost:8080> and, with [cloudflared](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/connections/connect-networks/) installed (`brew install cloudflared`), prints a temporary `https://….trycloudflare.com` link. The link changes every time and works while the command runs; Ctrl+C stops both.
- Without `--tunnel` it listens on this computer only. `--lan` also listens on the local network, `--port N` picks the port, `--afk S` sets the away timeout (default 60 seconds), and `--allow-origin URL` lets pages from one more address connect.
- On a local copy of the page, `?mpserver=ws://localhost:8080/ws` shows the Multiplayer buttons and points them at a local server, the way the site will once `MP_SERVER` in `prototype.html` is set. The override only works on localhost.

**Rooms.** Press Create a room and you get a four-letter code (consonants only, easy to read aloud) and a link with `?room=CODE` to copy or share. Friends open the link, or type the code. The browser keeps a private token for each room, so reloading or reopening the link takes back your name and seat; typing someone else's name does not. An empty room closes after 10 minutes.

**The lobby.** Pick one of four seats (each has a number and a colour: teal, pink, purple, orange) or stay standing to watch, and any seated player starts the race. Seated players share three settings: chill mode, bots in empty seats, and an optional seed. The starting order is drawn each race, so no seat is always in front. Your lab is drawn as yours and the others by name; the end card lists every lab, and Professor Postmortem tells the race from your side. Campaign, daily, achievements, ghosts, pause and the tuning knobs are off in multiplayer.

**Host and away players.** Whoever creates the room is the host. Between races the host can **Free seat** (the player moves to watching and can sit again) or **Remove** (the player leaves the room and cannot rejoin it). A player whose tab is hidden or closed shows as "(away)"; in the lobby, an away player loses their seat after a minute. During a race nothing changes: the lab keeps its last controls. If the host is away for 10 seconds or presses Leave room, the next player present becomes host; a host who just reloads stays host.

**How it works.** The server runs each room's race itself, with its own copy of the simulation section of `prototype.html` (the code `check/sim-check.mjs` loads), stepping at 1/60 s and sending the race state to every browser 30 times a second (about 2 KB each, so about 65 KB/s per player). Each browser sends its Run / Build, split, Back up, Floor it and jumps, and draws the race 0.1 s behind the newest state so motion stays smooth. A hidden tab cannot stall a race. In the simulation the only change is that each human lab can carry its own input and control-lag buffer (`l.hs`); one-player races are unchanged (the 300 reference games match). The server caps rooms, connections, people per room and messages per connection, and accepts sockets only from the site, localhost and its own address. `/health` returns room, race and connection counts.

**Limits.** Anyone with a room's link can join it, so share it only with the people you are racing. Remove is by browser token, so a removed player who clears their browser storage can come back as someone new. Controls reach the race after the network delay; chill mode (auto-jump, on by default) avoids timing jumps by hand over the internet. Tested: one four-player evening through a tunnel (the single-room version, 2026-10-30), plus browser tabs on one Mac for the room version; rooms have not yet been played on real phones or by people far from the server.

## Checks

`check/sim-check.mjs` replays 300 races (seeds 1 to 100 with chill on, chill off, and Steward alone) and compares each game's full result with `check/reference.json`. It needs only Node: `node experiments/pacing-the-frontier/check/sim-check.mjs`. When the simulation section of `prototype.html` (from `function $(id)` to `window.PF={`) is byte-identical to the recorded one, it skips the replay, so UI changes cost nothing. When race results change on purpose, run it with `--write` and commit `reference.json` with the change. It also keeps the simulation replayable in any browser: it fails if the simulation section uses `Math.sin`, `cos`, `log`, `exp`, `pow` and the like (which can differ in the last bit between engines; the simulation uses its own `dsin`, `dcos`, `dlog` and `dexp`), `Date`, `performance`, `crypto` or `**`, and it fails if `SIM_HASH` (the sha256 of the simulation section, which the page puts in every input log's header as `sim`) is stale; `--write` updates it. `check/replay-check.mjs` (about 1 s) replays recorded input logs at the live game's 1/60 s step and compares each result exactly: the 22 stored runs in `check/replay-reference.json` (six dates, all three tiers, chill off and on, chill switched mid-race, two players who hammer the jump key), and the same 22 recorded fresh. It also checks that logging jumps the old way (without `recAfter`) is caught on the hammering players, that the `d` functions stay within 1e-12 of `Math`, and that headers carry `sim`. It is Node against Node, so it proves the recorder and replayer agree, not that Safari computes the same race: `node experiments/pacing-the-frontier/check/replay-check.mjs --html` writes `check/replay-browser.html`, a single page that replays the stored runs in whatever browser opens it and lists OK or DIFFERENT per run plus every finish time, to compare across Safari, Chrome and Firefox. If the simulation changes on purpose, run `replay-check.mjs --write --html` and commit the new reference. `check/content-check.mjs` (about 6 s) checks that campaign progress survives a seed change, that every daily twist lowers the custom bot's wins on all three tiers, chill off and on, on 100 daily seeds from 2029-01-01 (bot play only), that appending a briefing moves no earlier date's briefing, and that every `BRIEFINGS`, `TWISTS` and `ACH_LIST` entry is well formed (known kind, twist in `TWISTS` with knobs inside their ranges, https links, three answers, a valid right answer), and that "you raced like" still names each rival kind as itself in at least 32 of 40 held-out runs (seeds 20001 to 20040, chill on). `check/mp-check.mjs` (about 10 s, Node 22 or later) starts the multiplayer server on a spare port and drives it with test clients: rooms and codes, two races at once, controls reaching the lab, host Free seat and Remove, the away timeout, reconnecting by token, host hand-over and the Origin check. `.github/workflows/pacing-check.yml` runs all four on pushes and pull requests that touch this folder.

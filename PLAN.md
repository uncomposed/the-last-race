# The Last Race — implementation plan

## Active implementation: playability revision, 14 October 2026

This section supersedes the delivery order below where it conflicts. Sections 1–7 retain the original product constraints and implementation history. The October 14 playability report establishes an owner-assisted completed campaign, not an independent usability result or the full fun gate.

**Product objective:** run an AI lab whose technology is imperfectly understood, while customers, competitors, resource commitments and deployment consequences make waiting costly. Preserve three strategic interventions, competent delegated operations, separate company/societal outcomes, replay determinism and fog of war. New state earns its place by changing a decision, an investigable uncertainty or a meaningful sacrifice.

### Model and contracts

| Layer | Contract for the next increment |
|---|---|
| Resources | Cash, quarterly compute, future capacity and intervention slots remain separate. Serving, routine work and strategic work use one ledger; resolution revenue cannot finance prior commitments. |
| Assets | Persistent model versions, task deployments and existing facilities. Training creates a candidate; an old candidate remains usable. |
| Knowledge | Hidden, seeded model properties are stable. Evaluations estimate performance for a specified task, harness and duration. Repeating a suite is not independent confirmation. Task reliability is not evidence of containment safety. |
| Operations | Standing mandates govern cash reserves, routine priorities and forecast capacity. They cannot authorize free strategic training, launches or treaties. |
| Actors | Companies serve a shared, bounded customer market using the same resource rules. Policies use their own observations, never model truth. Governments retain their declared limited implementation until the later contract milestone. |
| Consequences | Active deployments consume resources and create continuing exposure. Incidents identify a deployment, observed consequences and costly responses. Political milestones do not reveal hidden information before a voluntary conclusion. |
| Evidence | Technical checks, paired strategy comparisons and human engagement are distinct gates. Synthetic quantities remain labeled assumptions. |

### R2 decision and timing contract

At opening, finish projects and admit evaluation results, then produce a public brief and routine plan. The player can evaluate a model, commission training, select/change a deployment, secure operations, fund/build, change a mandate or respond. Evaluations specify their model, task, harness, duration and independent suite; results arrive at the next opening. Deployment changes take effect at resolution. Existing operations can throttle to affordable capacity rather than trap the player in an impossible quarter. Show requested versus funded capacity and forgone service. Resolve resource-feasible work, allocate shared demand, collect service revenue and record deployment evidence/incidents. Do not claim exact future revenue when customers, model performance or competitors are uncertain.

Political milestones are dated records with an administrative conclude/continue decision that spends no intervention. Continue advances once without rerunning resolution; conclude alone permits the terminal reveal. Physical loss-of-control, insolvency and the declared horizon retain terminal handling. Explicit scored scenarios retain their announced ending objectives.

### Acceptance, falsification and stopping rules

- Repair regressions: exhausted parent funding, saturated regional allocation, allowed construction under a pledge, observed training that violates it, zero-return launches, surplus compute, persistent mandates and continuation with unfinished assets.
- Evidence value: two unchanged models; evaluation changes task-specific model preference, another task favors the other candidate; omit the evaluation and the justified preference disappears. Include misleading evidence and reject repeated-suite evidence farming.
- Resource coupling: serving competes with evaluation/training for compute; vendor serving pays recurring charges; no demand means no guaranteed revenue; an unjustified facility remains a bad investment. Compare product, frontier, integrator and cooperation strategies under matched seeds without requiring equal outcomes.
- Consequences: the same deployment incident admits suspension, restriction or replacement; continued exposure has modeled consequences. Ordinary reliability failures do not automatically become containment failures.
- Technical: atomic rejection, command idempotency, exact replay/checkpoint recovery, hidden-state perturbation of every new public selector and preview, real CLI/MCP commands, and Node/Chromium/WebKit equality. Hash all new transition modules. Preserve old artifacts with their matching engine.
- Human: owner plus two non-specialists can explain the next routine choice, identify two meaningful sacrifices, select a model using evidence, and finish or voluntarily conclude. Record replay intent and the different strategy they would try. No source edits during advertised play.

Stop breadth expansion if evaluations become compulsory clicks, a strategy is automatic, players need source inspection for ordinary decisions, mandates need repeated correction, or technical boundaries fail. The prior failure being caught is confusing broad feature coverage and successful command execution with an understandable, enjoyable strategy game.

## 1. Product and decision model

Build an educational strategy game for curious non-specialists, beginning with local MCP play. Start in Q1 2025, branch from history, and report lab viability, influence, and societal outcomes separately.

**The company runs itself; the player intervenes three times per quarter.**

| Tier | Decision maker | Player influence |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic interventions | Player, within three moves | Direct choices |
| Routine company operations | Belief-driven company policy | Override a plan item or belief |
| Other actors | Rival and government policies | Evidence, announcements, deals, observable behavior |
| World | Scenario mechanisms and seeded uncertainty | Preparation and exposure |

Each quarter, the company proposes a routine plan with costs, expected benefits, and short explanations grounded in its beliefs. Delegated operations include leasing capacity, routine chip purchases, baseline safety spending, and routine product improvements.

Use the same policy machinery for the player’s company, rivals, governments, and simulation bots. Policies score options under actor beliefs and objective weights over revenue, capability lead, safety reputation, and mission. Switching costs create organizational inertia.

Every decision belongs to one tier. Distinguish routine upgrades from strategic Ship interventions so delegation cannot bypass the move limit.

### Player interventions

| Move | Choice | Visibility |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Region, scale, power source, concentrated or distributed facilities | Physical |
| Train | Scale, pretraining or RL, two capability focuses | Covert; frontier power draw also creates a physical signal |
| Ship | Product launch or strategic upgrade; harness using own or third-party models | Public |
| Fund | External funding or parent-company budget | Public |
| Secure | Security, control, or alignment spending beyond baseline | Covert |
| Investigate | Evals, interpretability, studies, or rival intelligence | Covert |
| Announce | Structured public claim, truthful or deceptive | Public |
| Override a belief | Impose a company conviction for four quarters | Internal |
| Respond | Force a feasible response to flagged evidence | Internal |

Plan-item overrides cost one move. A once-per-run, human-approved wildcard replaces one normal move. All share the three-slot budget, without partial slots.

Concealment is a paid modifier on Build, Train, or Secure.

Individual hiring, model architecture details, per-product pricing, lawsuits, marketing, multiplayer diplomacy, and playing as a country or AI remain outside scope.

## 2. Beliefs, evidence, and world mechanisms

### Independent grid beliefs

Use **20 support points per learnable constant**, displayed as five contiguous probability bins. Store grid support and display-bin boundaries in scenario data; aggregate probability mass rather than averaging probabilities.

Maintain independent posteriors and enumerate every grid point when updating. Updates are exact enumeration under the finite independent model, subject to ordinary floating-point rounding—not sampling-based inference. No sequential Monte Carlo, exact rational arithmetic, or BigInt.

Beliefs cover world quantities, derived takeoff timelines, rival racing intentions, capability, safety posture, and credibility. Distinguish latent constants from derived beliefs.

Evidence likelihoods incorporate ambiguity and credibility. Topic-specific receptivity modifies admitted evidence through a deterministic mixture with uninformative evidence. Multiconstant evidence uses explicit per-constant likelihood tables under the independence assumption.

Revisit joint inference only if documented playtests show that missing correlations materially change decisions or undermine explanations.

**Overrides:** keep the evidence-based posterior separate from the conviction used by company policy. An override lasts four quarters, including the current quarter; evidence continues updating underneath it. Expiry restores the current evidence-based belief. Replacing or ending an override costs one move, without a surcharge for disagreement.

### Three evidence gates

1. **Detect:** Public signals are observed without a roll. Physical and covert detection depends on signal size, concealment, and observer intelligence. Internal actions require a separate leak mechanism to produce external evidence.
2. **Interpret:** Evidence ambiguity, source credibility, and actor receptivity determine the update.
3. **Act:** Updated expected benefits or risks must overcome switching costs and organizational inertia.

Show the player company’s receptivity and switching costs with explanations. Rival traits remain uncertain. The feed distinguishes discounted, noted, and acted-on evidence; undetected evidence appears only in the final reveal.

`Respond` selects detected, flagged evidence, reassesses it at full receptivity while retaining ambiguity and credibility, and commits a chosen feasible response despite organizational inertia. Replace its earlier evidence contribution rather than counting it twice. Actual financial and operational costs remain payable. Respond does not cancel a belief override.

False positives arise independently of actual hidden actions. Absence counts as evidence only after a defined observation opportunity. Repeated claims cannot manufacture independent evidence.

Announcements record speaker, claim, target, and time. Public exposure does not imply acceptance. Receivers weight claims by credibility; later corroboration or contradiction changes credibility.

### Risk, geography, and actors

Preserve the mitigation ceiling:

`risk = action risk × (1 − mitigation)`

Mitigation is bounded by hidden control effectiveness. Model escape attempts, success, and detection separately, including warning shots, undetected failures, detected incidents, and hidden breakouts discovered later.

The full MVP includes 12 scenario-defined regions with power availability, costs, delays, hazards, and jurisdiction. MCP Build commands use region IDs; the later map presents the same decisions.

The complete roster contains a DeepMind-inspired playable company and rivals inspired by OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, DeepSeek, and xAI/SpaceX, plus limited US/China government actors.

Support eight ending families:

- Safe lead, described as assessed control rather than certified safety.
- Multipolar balance.
- Coordinated slowdown.
- Winner takes all.
- Loss of control.
- Left behind.
- Failed slowdown through consequential defection.
- Verification broken when a new paradigm invalidates an established verification regime.

Store ending conditions and overlap precedence in scenario data. Defection and verification failure must emerge from their respective mechanisms, not generic failure rolls.

## 3. Engine, interfaces, and numerical contract

Use one TypeScript engine shared by MCP, a plain CLI printer, simulation bots, and the later browser client. Start with small validated scenario records. Add YAML compilation and bounded expression support incrementally.

Separate authoritative world state, observations, beliefs, plans, commands, and causal traces.

### Quarter execution

- Apply opening upkeep, existing-project progress, and observations.
- Process evidence and generate the proposed routine plan.
- Accept up to three player interventions and show resulting plan revisions.
- Have rivals decide through the same policy machinery without seeing private player intentions.
- Commit a resource-feasible combination of routine decisions and interventions.
- Resolve effects, record evidence and gate outcomes, and evaluate endings.

Routine operations and interventions share one resource ledger. Overrides replace or veto identified plan items. Conflicts require a visible correction before commitment.

New projects first progress in the following quarter. Ordinary resolution evidence informs the next plan.

### Public interfaces

Provide run creation, save/resume, `get_view`, `get_plan`, `override_plan_item`, `get_beliefs`, `get_feed`, `get_rivals`, `list_actions`, `take_action`, `override_belief`, `respond_to_evidence`, `end_turn`, `explain`, `get_rules`, and wildcard proposal tools.

Commands carry actor identity, idempotency keys, and expected revisions. Invalid or stale commands cannot partially mutate state.

Tools return structured data. Narration takes numbers from tool results. The CLI presents the same player-visible information without a language model.

Action previews include visibility and per-observer detection estimates without revealing hidden observer traits.

### Numerical contract: settled in Stage 1

Use IEEE doubles for authoritative numerical computation, with these restrictions:

- Arithmetic is limited to `+`, `-`, `*`, and `/`, with fixed evaluation and reduction order.
- Nonlinear effects and distribution sampling use versioned lookup tables or piecewise-linear functions.
- Prohibit implementation-dependent math such as native `exp`, `log`, and non-integer powers.
- Sort iteration inputs explicitly; resolve equal policy scores using stable action IDs.
- Use integer fixed-point only where exact threshold equality matters, with declared units, rounding, and safe-integer bounds.
- Reject non-finite values, define zero-denominator handling, and canonicalize negative zero during serialization.
- Normalize posterior weights after each update. If all likelihood-weighted mass is zero, retain the prior, flag the incompatible observation, and record the diagnostic.

Canonical serialization and hashing do not substitute for deterministic transitions. Test numerical boundary cases across JavaScript runtimes during Stage 1.

Use keyed, versioned randomness for world events and observations. Grid inference requires no randomness.

Replay identity includes engine/scenario hashes, numerical-contract version, model variant, seed, committed decisions, overrides, and accepted wildcard effects. Preserve compatible replay artifacts.

Private causal traces remain separate from permitted explanations. Offline saves contain recoverable hidden information; competitive anti-cheat is outside scope.

## 5. Verification, defaults, and stopping rules

Required checks cover:

- Cross-runtime transition and decision-trace equality, including threshold and tie cases.
- Enumerated grid updates, five-bin aggregation, normalization, zero-likelihood handling, and evidence deduplication.
- Missed evidence, discounted evidence, belief change without action, and effective Respond intervention.
- Four-quarter override expiry, continued underlying learning, and shared move accounting.
- Knowledge boundaries across views, plans, explanations, errors, and previews.
- Announcements, credibility changes, false positives, monitored absence, and concealed signals.
- Resource feasibility, duplicate commands, delayed projects, and terminal-state handling.
- Reproducible witnesses for all eight ending families.
- Paired-seed comparisons across policies, priors, and mechanism variants without forcing equal win rates.

Retain parody names, Apache-2.0 licensing, Priors as the default information mode, a 2025–2031 horizon, and approximately 45-minute eventual browser sessions. Preserve human acceptance of local wildcards and the eventual $200 monthly hosted-ruling ceiling.

Empirical quantities generally use distributions. Separate evidence, assumptions, actor beliefs, and game conventions. Later historical evidence can calibrate mechanisms without automatically entering actors’ January 2025 knowledge.

For initial false-positive testing, use one rumor opportunity per observer-quarter and an explicitly exploratory seeded propensity drawn from `{0%, 1%, 2%, 3%, 5%}`. Tune against useful investigation and manageable feed noise.

Stop progression when determinism, knowledge boundaries, required ending pathways, or the applicable fun gate fails. Add modeling complexity only when a concrete play or validation failure warrants it. When plausible assumptions reverse a strategic conclusion, expose that dependency.
